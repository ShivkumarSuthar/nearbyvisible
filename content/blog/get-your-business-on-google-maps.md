When someone searches "near me", Google shows a map with a few businesses on it. Those businesses get most of the calls. Getting on that map starts with one free tool: your **Google Business Profile**.

This guide walks you through it, step by step.

## 1. Check if your business is already listed

Search for your business name and area on Google Maps. Sometimes a customer or Google itself has already added it.

- **If you find it**, look for "Own this business?" or "Claim this business" and follow the steps.
- **If you do not find it**, you will create a new profile.
- **If someone else controls it**, you can request access through Google. Google contacts the current owner and decides based on their reply.

## 2. Create the profile

Go to Google Business Profile and sign in with a Google account you will keep for years. Use a business email if you have one, not a personal account you might lose.

Then add:

1. **Business name**, exactly as it appears on your board. Do not add extra keywords. Google can suspend profiles for that.
2. **Primary category**, the single most accurate description of what you do. This decides which searches you appear in.
3. **Location or service area.** If customers visit you, add your address. If you visit them, add the areas you serve.
4. **Phone number and website**, if you have one.

## 3. Verify it

Google needs to confirm you are the real owner. Depending on your business, it may ask for a video of your premises, a phone call, a text message or a postcard. Follow the option Google offers you.

> Verification can take anything from minutes to a few weeks, and the method is chosen by Google. Keep your details consistent while you wait.

## 4. Complete every section

A complete profile gets more clicks than a half-empty one. Fill in:

- **Opening hours**, including holiday hours.
- **Services or products**, with short descriptions.
- **A business description** that says what you do and who you help.
- **Photos**: your shop or office from outside, inside, your team and your work. Real photos beat stock images.
- **Attributes** like "wheelchair accessible" or "online appointments", where they apply.

## 5. Get your first reviews

Reviews tell new customers they can trust you. Ask happy customers to leave one, using the review link from your profile. Never buy or write fake reviews. Google removes them and can penalise the profile.

## 6. Keep it active

Google favours profiles that look alive. Every month:

- Add a few new photos.
- Reply to every review, good or bad.
- Post an update or offer.
- Check your hours and phone number are still correct.

## The short version

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Find | Search for your business on Maps |
| Create or claim | Use a Google account you will keep |
| Verify | Follow the method Google offers |
| Complete | Hours, services, photos, description |
| Reviews | Ask real customers, reply to all |
| Maintain | Update something every month |
